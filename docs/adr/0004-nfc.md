# ADR 0004: Phone-to-phone NFC is Android only; QR stays the universal path

**Status:** accepted (Day 11) · **Owner:** mobile

## Context
zysygy.de sells "Tap to Pay". Zahln moves money account to account (SEPA Instant), not over card rails, so the
contactless step only has to carry what a QR carries: the signed intent reference (`<id>.<sig>`), never an amount
or an account.

What a third-party app may do with NFC, as of iOS 26 / Android 16:

| | Android | iPhone |
|---|---|---|
| Read tags / ISO 7816 cards | Yes (reader mode) | Yes, Core NFC (with the NFC entitlement) |
| Emulate a card (HCE) | Yes, `HostApduService` with our own AID | Only in the EEA, iOS 17.4+, with Apple's HCE entitlement: a commercial agreement, payment-provider status and an app review |
| Accept contactless *cards* | via a PSP SDK | Tap to Pay on iPhone, approved PSPs only, for cards, not our references |

## Decision
- **The till emulates, the payer reads.** ZYSYGY on Android runs a `HostApduService` (proprietary AID
  `F0 5A 41 48 4C 4E 01`, category `other`) while a QR is open; Zahln on Android reads it in reader mode on the scan
  screen and gets the same pay URL the QR holds. Both then use the normal pay flow (server amount, PIN/Face ID,
  idempotency).
- Category `other`, not `payment`: `payment` would make Zahln compete to be the phone's default wallet.
- The service is **disabled in the manifest** and only enabled while a till offers a payment, so the payer app
  (same module) never answers.
- **iOS:** the module isn't linked at all (`react-native.config.js`), and the Tap to Pay button is hidden. QR is the
  path. When we're ready for the EEA HCE entitlement, the iPhone *till* could emulate too; an iPhone *payer* can
  already read with Core NFC, which is the cheaper first step.
- The `nfc_pay` kill switch hides the button, stops the till offering, and stops the payer reading.

## Consequences
- Needs two real Android phones to demo (emulators have no NFC). Without them: code walkthrough and a recording.
- A relay attack can forward the APDU, but the reference is the same capability a photo of the QR already gives,
  and paying still needs the payer's own PIN/biometrics and shows the server's merchant and amount.
